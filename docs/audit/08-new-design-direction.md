# Caline Website Audit — Stage 8: New Professional Design Direction

**Project:** Caline Chocolate  
**Repository:** `Mo31D/Caline`  
**Research date:** 2026-09-23

## Goal

Define a concrete design direction for a new Caline website using:

- verified company/manufacturing research;
- historical Caline packaging/identity;
- the authentic kangaroo/Caline logo;
- the real product families recovered so far;
- current premium-chocolate UX conventions;
- a design language that does **not** look like a generic “AI luxury” website.

---

# 1. Core creative direction

## Working concept

# **CALINE — QUIET CRAFT**

A contemporary premium chocolate brand whose luxury comes from:
- craft;
- material quality;
- product precision;
- manufacturing provenance;
- restraint.

The site should not try to look expensive by filling every surface with:
- black;
- gold gradients;
- glass effects;
- chocolate splashes;
- decorative serif typography.

Instead:

> **Caline’s gold script/kangaroo logo is the jewellery.  
> The website around it should be calm, tactile and precise.**

---

# 2. Strategic positioning of the website

Caline should not be presented as another generic online gift shop.

The strongest externally supported position is:

> a GCC chocolate manufacturer with a documented Hamriyah Free Zone production base, handmade Belgian-chocolate positioning, bean-to-bar process history and premium regional retail heritage.

The site should balance two identities:

### Consumer brand
Premium chocolates, gift boxes, pralines, truffles.

### Maker/manufacturer
Real factory, real process, real production capability.

That second layer is what can make Caline more credible and distinctive.

---

# 3. Benchmark lessons — without copying competitors

## Mirzam

Mirzam demonstrates how a UAE chocolate brand can turn **process/origin/story** into brand value rather than relying only on “luxury” language.

Useful lesson:
- make the craft story tangible;
- let packaging and ingredients tell stories.

Do **not** copy Mirzam’s illustrated Spice Route aesthetic. That identity belongs to Mirzam.

Reference:  
https://mirzam.com/

Packaging case study:  
https://backbonebranding.com/works/mirzam-origins-collection/

---

## Neuhaus

Neuhaus shows strong catalogue hierarchy:
- collection;
- chocolate type;
- product type;
- gifting;
- provenance/trust content.

It also places factual trust signals such as manufacturing origin and cacao sourcing alongside commerce.

Useful lesson:
> premium ecommerce still needs very clear taxonomy and product facts.

Reference:  
https://www.neuhauschocolates.com/en_BE/shop/

---

## Läderach

Läderach keeps the commercial structure simple:
- Milk;
- Dark;
- White;
- Nuts;
- Fruits;
- gifts;
then builds sensory/craft storytelling around it.

Useful lesson:
> users should be able to understand the shop in seconds.

Reference:  
https://laderach.com/uk-en/

# 4. What Caline should NOT imitate

## Do not fabricate European heritage

Caline has a Belgian-chocolate positioning, but the strongest verified business story is connected to the UAE/GCC.

The design should **not** pretend to be:
- a 19th-century Belgian maison;
- an old Parisian chocolatier;
- a faux-French heritage brand.

The French/script character already exists naturally in the logo. The website should contrast that with modern Gulf confidence.

## Do not imitate Dubai “black + gold luxury”

A full black website with glowing gold borders, marble, reflections and excessive animations would make Caline look generated rather than established.

Use gold sparingly.

---

# 5. Visual system

## Primary surfaces

### Warm Ivory
Main background.

Purpose:
- allows dark chocolate photography to stand out;
- gives packaging room;
- avoids sterile pure-white ecommerce.

Approximate direction:
`#F5F0E8` / `#F8F4EE`

Final values should be tuned from real packaging assets.

### Deep Cocoa
Primary dark text/surface.

Approximate direction:
`#241713` / `#2B1A15`

### Caline Gold
Derived from the real logo.

Use for:
- logo;
- fine keylines;
- tiny details;
- hover accents;
- selected metadata.

Do not use as large filled buttons everywhere.

### Caramel / Suede
Based on historical gift-box imagery.

Use in editorial/craft sections and material backgrounds.

---

# 6. Supporting material language

The physical/material mood should come from:

- warm paper;
- suede/tan gift-box material;
- cocoa;
- dark wood used sparingly;
- brushed/muted gold;
- ivory packaging;
- real chocolate texture.

Avoid:
- fake marble everywhere;
- glossy black;
- floating 3D spheres;
- glassmorphism;
- liquid chocolate CGI.

---

# 7. Typography direction

The logo remains the only decorative script.

## Arabic

Choose one sophisticated, highly readable Arabic family suitable for:
- editorial headings;
- product information;
- UI.

Desired character:
- elegant;
- contemporary;
- not geometric/tech;
- not overly calligraphic.

## Latin

Use a restrained family with strong product/editorial readability.

Two viable design routes:

### Route A — modern editorial
A refined serif for editorial headlines + neutral sans for UI.

### Route B — quieter and more contemporary
One premium grotesk/humanist sans across interface, allowing the Caline wordmark to provide contrast.

### Recommendation

For this brand, **Route B is safer**.

It avoids competing with the script logo and reduces the risk of a faux-European visual identity.

---

# 8. Logo treatment

## Desktop header

- kangaroo + Caline mark centred or left/right according to language;
- modest size;
- generous clear space;
- no brown box behind it;
- no gradient glow.

## Mobile

Use:
- full wordmark when space permits;
- kangaroo/icon-only version only if a proper source/vector can be prepared.

## Motion

A very subtle logo reveal is acceptable.

Do not animate the kangaroo jumping across the page. That would trivialise the premium identity.

---

# 9. Kangaroo as a design device

The kangaroo is distinctive enough to become a subtle secondary brand motif.

Possible uses:
- blind-embossed watermark on an ivory section;
- tiny footer/section marker;
- packaging-pattern detail;
- line-art crop;
- hover accent on selected brand-story elements.

Avoid:
- cartoon illustration;
- repeated kangaroos;
- animated mascot behaviour.

---

# 10. Homepage wireframe

## Section 1 — Hero

### Visual
One real Caline product/packaging photograph.

Prefer:
- an open assortment box;
- dark chocolates + authentic packaging;
- controlled warm studio light.

### Copy structure

Eyebrow:
**CALINE CHOCOLATE**

Headline:
short, 1–2 lines.

Supporting copy:
one factual sentence.

Primary CTA:
**Explore Chocolate**

Secondary CTA:
**Our Craft**

No carousel.

---

## Section 2 — Collection index

A clean 3–4 card grid:

- Assorted Chocolates
- Dark Chocolate
- Truffles
- Gift Boxes

Only show categories confirmed as current at launch.

Cards should use real imagery with restrained type overlays or captions below.

---

## Section 3 — “Made, not merely sold”

This is the key differentiation section.

Split editorial layout:

**Left:** factory/process image  
**Right:** concise manufacturing story.

Potential factual structure:
- Hamriyah Free Zone;
- chocolate production;
- moulding;
- packaging;
- bean-to-bar history.

This section should make a visitor understand that Caline is a maker/manufacturer.

---

## Section 4 — Signature presentation

Large close-up image:
- gift box;
- individual pralines;
- texture/presentation.

Minimal copy.

The purpose is emotion and product desirability, not another information block.

---

## Section 5 — Craft sequence

Three concise steps, only when final wording is verified.

Example visual model:
1. Chocolate
2. Craft
3. Finish

Do not publish an exact country-by-country supply chain until confirmed.

---

## Section 6 — Gifting

Show gift boxes/occasion presentation.

No fake prices before current catalogue is supplied.

CTA:
**Discover Gifting**

---

## Section 7 — Brand history / regional footprint

A restrained timeline or short narrative.

Only verified milestones:
- documented factory history;
- historical regional boutiques/sites;
- current active locations after confirmation.

Avoid inflated numbers.

---

## Section 8 — Contact / B2B

If manufacturing/wholesale services are confirmed:

> For retail, gifting and business enquiries.

Two clear routes:
- Customer / Retail
- Business / Manufacturing

---

# 11. Product listing design

## Grid

Desktop:
3 or 4 columns depending on imagery.

Tablet:
2–3.

Mobile:
2 compact cards where readable, otherwise 1.

## Card anatomy

- image;
- collection/type;
- product name;
- weight or piece count;
- price;
- availability;
- one restrained action.

No:
- huge hover animations;
- multiple badges;
- fake star ratings;
- long card descriptions.

---

# 12. Product-detail page direction

## Above fold

Two-column layout:

### Media
- front packshot;
- open-box shot;
- piece/detail shot;
- packaging/back label.

### Information
- exact name;
- concise descriptor;
- weight/piece count;
- price;
- variant;
- CTA.

## Below fold

Accordion or structured sections:
- Flavours / fillings
- Ingredients
- Allergens
- Storage
- Delivery
- Product story

Food information should be highly legible, not hidden beneath decorative copy.

---

# 13. Manufacturing page direction

This page can become one of the strongest parts of the site.

## Structure

1. Large real factory image
2. Caline manufacturing introduction
3. Process sequence
4. Hamriyah Free Zone provenance
5. Quality/capabilities
6. B2B capability if confirmed
7. enquiry CTA

## Visual style

More documentary than commercial:
- machinery;
- moulding;
- finishing;
- hands/process;
- packaging;
- factory environment.

No stock factory photos.

---

# 14. Our Story page

Should be shorter and more factual than the current invented narrative.

Recommended:
- identity;
- documented starting period;
- factory;
- regional expansion;
- craft philosophy.

Use a small timeline only where dates are proven.

---

# 15. Mobile design

Mobile should not be a compressed desktop design.

## Header
- logo;
- menu;
- cart only if real commerce exists.

## Hero
- image-first;
- 35–50% less text than desktop;
- CTA visible without scrolling excessively.

## Product cards
- touch targets;
- no hover-dependent details;
- product information remains visible.

## Editorial sections
Alternate carefully but avoid constant zig-zag layouts.

---

# 16. Motion direction

Motion should support tactility, not spectacle.

Acceptable:
- very slow image reveal;
- subtle mask/clip transition;
- 150–250ms UI hover/focus;
- gentle product image scale on hover;
- small line/keyline movement.

Avoid:
- parallax on every section;
- fake 3D chocolate rotation;
- animated drips;
- cursor effects;
- scroll hijacking.

---

# 17. Photography art direction

## Hero photography

- real product;
- packaging included;
- directional soft light;
- natural shadow;
- tactile surface;
- no random props.

## Product packshots

Standardised:
- same camera angle;
- same background;
- same crop scale;
- colour-correct chocolate.

## Editorial

Close-ups of:
- moulding;
- cutting;
- filling;
- texture;
- packaging;
- hands/process.

A premium chocolate site succeeds or fails largely on photography consistency.

---

# 18. Page background rhythm

A recommended sequence:

1. Warm ivory
2. Product-white/light
3. Cocoa dark editorial section
4. Warm ivory
5. Tan/material feature
6. Warm ivory footer transition

Use dark sections intentionally, not as the entire website.

---

# 19. Button system

## Primary
Deep cocoa fill + ivory text.

## Secondary
Transparent + cocoa keyline.

## Text link
Underlined or arrow-led editorial link.

Gold should generally **not** be the filled primary button.

That keeps the gold logo from becoming visually cheap through overuse.

---

# 20. Cards and borders

Use:
- thin hairline borders;
- minimal radius or subtle 2–6px radius;
- little/no drop shadow;
- soft tonal background differentiation.

Avoid large rounded SaaS-style cards.

This is a physical product brand, not an app dashboard.

---

# 21. The one distinctive idea

The design needs one memorable signature.

## Proposed signature:
### **The Caline Line**

A very thin gold/brown line derived from the flowing stroke of the Caline wordmark.

It can:
- guide the eye between editorial sections;
- form understated section dividers;
- trace around a product-image crop;
- occasionally resolve into a tiny kangaroo mark.

The idea is subtle enough to be premium and specific enough to Caline.

It should be implemented as vector/CSS geometry, not as a decorative squiggle repeated everywhere.

---

# 22. How this differs from the current site

| Current | Proposed |
|---|---|
| generic chocolate template | brand-specific editorial system |
| stock cakes/doughnuts | real Caline chocolate |
| brown/orange heavy UI | ivory/cocoa/gold restraint |
| three navigation systems | one global header |
| carousel hero | one decisive hero |
| fake testimonials | provenance and real proof |
| unsupported statistics | documented facts |
| fake ecommerce | catalogue or real checkout |

| chocolate drips/splashes | tactile product/material photography |

---

# 23. Content needed before visual production

The design can begin using placeholders for layout, but the final production build should wait for:

- confirmed current product list;
- approved product images;
- active locations;
- approved contact channels;
- current business services;
- confirmed company-history dates;
- confirmed manufacturing claims.

---

# 24. Recommended design target

The final experience should feel like:

> **a real premium GCC chocolate maker with a European craft vocabulary — not a European brand imitation and not a generic Dubai luxury template.**

Caline already has a useful distinctive asset: its historical kangaroo/script mark.

The design’s job is to create enough restraint around that identity for it to feel valuable again.

---

# Stage 8 conclusion

The recommended redesign is not a visual reskin.

It is a shift toward:
- authentic product;
- proof of craft;
- quiet luxury;
- editorial clarity;
- modern GCC identity;
- one coherent commerce/manufacturing experience.
